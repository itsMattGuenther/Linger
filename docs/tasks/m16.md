issues (`docs/decisions.md`, "GitHub's issues are the work queue"). Matt, that
day: the real-world checks still marked owed here were ones nobody could run by
hand, and the app in daily use since has settled them. The marks are left as
they stood. What was never built is listed in `TASKS.md`, "Written down, not

friend's presentation from Windows on 2026-10-28. SPEC §4.20 is what it does, PROTOCOL §8

and not as a WebRTC track. Branch `feat/360-screen-sharing`. Numbers are `T-19xx`.

- 🟡 **T-1907 · Sharing from Linux and macOS** — **Linux works, from Matt's desktop
  (2026-10-25).** WebKitGTK couldn't do it: it reads a shared screen only as GPU buffers
  (DMA-BUF), and on Matt's two-GPU machine and in a container it never turned them into
  a picture. So on Linux the shell captures the screen itself
  (`src-tauri/src/screen_shell.rs`, `screen_shell/linux.rs`): the desktop's ScreenCast
  portal picks it, GStreamer reads the PipeWire stream in ordinary memory, scales it to
  fit 1080p and encodes VP8, and the page gets finished frames on a Tauri channel and
  sends them on the screen socket as usual (`lib/screen/shell.ts`). The size is asked
  for by name: asked as a range, Hyprland's portal gave a 4K monitor at 2×2. A preview
  of 480×270 JPEGs at 4 a second comes on another channel, made only while shown. Share
  is offered on Linux once the shell says the portal and GStreamer's PipeWire and VP8
  plugins are there. The packages bring PipeWire's GStreamer plugin (required on Arch,
  recommended in the .deb and .rpm, #377). The AppImage doesn't share: its bundled
  GStreamer has no PipeWire source, and a bundled PipeWire could disagree with the
  computer's. Checked on Matt's Omarchy desktop (a window, then his whole 4K screen,
  30 and 60 fps) and in a container with sway's portal. On 2026-10-25 the real app
  (a debug build with its pages inside) ran in that container on a virtual display,
  driven by an injected script through its own screens: joined the test server by
  invite, joined voice, Share at 30 fps, the preview shown, hidden and shown again,
  Stop, with the menu staying shut; a watcher on the test server saw the screen at its
  own 1280×720 the whole time. On 2026-10-26 Matt shared his whole 4K screen three
  times, at 30 and 60 fps, with the preview hidden and shown: the watcher got
  1920×1080 each time, and a window on another workspace, out of sight, which the
  watcher saw at 1920×1056. macOS is still Share-less: nobody has tried its picker.

- 🟡 **T-1911 · Watching in the real Linux app** (2026-10-27, #729, #730) — the first
  real watch from one Linux app to another failed two ways, and neither had been tried:
  the browser tests run Playwright's own WebKit, and the real-app check had no GPU
  decoders and a Chromium watcher. With NVIDIA's or VA-API's decoders installed, WebKitGTK
  took them and fed them input they refuse (#730). Startup now ranks the hardware H.264 and
  VP8 decoders out (`GST_PLUGIN_FEATURE_RANK`, `linux_startup.rs`), checked in the
  packaged app's web process by `linux-next-check.mjs`. On NVIDIA's legacy driver, with
  `GDK_GL=disable`, building any WebCodecs decoder aborted the page (#729). The shell
  now says so (`screen_watch_available`), and `playable()` never asks there. Both were
  reproduced in WebKitGTK 2.54.1 in a container with the machine's GPUs passed in. The
  #730 setting was confirmed by hand on the RTX 4090 machine. **Still to do:** the same
  watch through a release, on both machines.
- ✅ **T-1912 · Share's words, said plainly** (2026-10-27, #731, #732) — the black-share
  line and notice are whole sentences, saying what the watchers see, then what to do,
  then why: a presenter read the old ones out and they didn't come across. Share's
  choices are **High quality** (1080p, 60 fps) and **Low quality** (720p, 30 fps, 2.5
  Mbit/s), each with its size and speed on a second line (the kit `Menu`'s new
  `detail`). "Sharp text" had promised a sharpness a whole 4K screen, scaled to 1080p,
  never had. The page and the Linux shell keep the same sizes (`presets.ts`,
  `screen_shell/linux.rs` `speed`).
- ✅ **T-1913 · Watching tested where Linux people watch** (2026-10-27, after #729 and
  #730) — 0.5.2's package checks ran Ubuntu 22.04's WebKit, with its GPU path off, and
  decoded only VP8, so neither failure could show. `linux-next-check.mjs` now asks the
  app whether it plays a screen, as the app does, and checks the answer fits how it
  was started. With `--webkit-gpu`, VP8 (and H.264 with `--h264`) must play; with
  `--gdk-gl-disable` it must refuse, and not crash. `scripts/linux-arch-check.sh` runs
  both on Arch's current WebKit in a container, in CI. With `--gpu` it hands over a
  real computer's graphics for the release step (`docs/releasing.md`). Proved both
  ways: it fails on 0.5.2's two bugs ("VP8 doesn't play: Decode error" with NVIDIA's
  decoders back in reach, and the web process dying under `GDK_GL=disable`), and passes
  on the fixes, with the RTX 4090 and Radeon 610M's decoders in the container.
- 🟡 **T-1915 · A sound when somebody starts watching** (Matt, 2026-10-27, #735) — only
  the person sharing hears it, a cue of its own (`watcher` in `chimes.ts`), from the list
  window whichever window the share runs in, at most once a minute for each person and
  never for a reconnect, a replay or the list a share starts with (`WatchCues`,
  `lib/sound-events.ts`). While you share, a voice join waits two seconds, so a Watch from
  outside voice rings this alone. Its own switch, on by default, which quiet hours leave
  alone. Tests: `sound-events.test.ts`, `sound-player.test.ts`, `gateway.voice.test.ts`,
  `next-list-window.spec.ts`, `next-settings.spec.ts`, `chime-onset.spec.ts`. **Still to

- 🟡 **T-1914 · A Linux share made on the graphics card** (2026-10-27, #733) — sharing
  from Linux spun the fans up. Measured through wlroots' portal on a busy 4K screen at
  60 fps: the share took 1.9 cores, 1.1 of them VP8, and VP8 managed 3.9 of its 6
  Mbit/s. The shell now finds a working H.264 encoder on the card once a run
  (`nvh264enc`, `vah264enc`, `h264_encoder`) and offers H.264 first; the share then took
  1.0 core and kept to 6 Mbit/s. Each format is encoded only while the server says a
  watcher wants it (`screen_share_want`), so nothing is encoded with nobody watching.
  A share whose card encoder fails goes back to VP8 for the rest of the run, and a
  window too small for the card's encoder is VP8 alone. **Still to do:** asking the
  portal for only the share's frame rate (`max-framerate`) saved 0.2 cores at Low
  quality, but a range made wlroots' portal send 2 frames a second, so it needs a try
  on Hyprland's portal first; and shrinking a 4K picture on the GPU for portals that
  don't shrink it themselves.
- 🟡 **T-1916 · The computer's sound** (Matt, 2026-10-27, #736) — a share can carry what
  the computer plays, never the call: the Windows shell captures it with WASAPI's process
  loopback, leaving out Linger's own process and the WebView2 processes it starts
  (`src-tauri/src/screen_sound/windows.rs`), and cuts it into 20 ms Opus packets at
  128 kbit/s, stereo (`screen_sound/pack.rs`). The page stamps them on the frames' clock and
  sends them on the share's socket only while the server's `want` says somebody watching
  asked (`lib/screen/share.ts`). The server passes them to the watchers who asked, held
  to the share's ceiling, never waiting for a key frame, dropped rather than queued for a
  watcher whose line is full (`gateway/screens.rs`, PROTOCOL §8 "The computer's sound").
  Desktop apps play it in the call through the chosen speakers, in stereo, 80 to 160 ms
  behind (`voice/device.rs` `ScreenLane`); a phone with WebCodecs' Opus plays it in the
  page (`lib/screen/listen.ts`). Share's menu has the tick, off until turned on; the box a
  speaker; a watched screen a speaker and, on a computer, a volume slider, all kept on that
  device. Tests: the header in `linger-core`, the board's and `tests/screen.rs`, the
  packetizer and the stereo mix in the shell, a Windows-only loopback test on CI's
  Windows runner, `sound.test.ts`, and the browser tests (`screen-pipeline.spec.ts`,
  `next-screen.spec.ts`). **Still to do:** hear it for real, a Windows share of a video
  watched on Windows, Linux and a phone, with someone talking in the call, to check
  nobody's voice comes back and the sound keeps up with the picture; and on an iPhone,
  whose web engine may have no Opus decoder. **Linux sharing sound** is later work: the
  desktop's screen-sharing portal gives no sound, and a PipeWire capture of what's
  playing would need Linger's own output streams found by their node and left out (a
  filter or a null sink), so the call never goes back out.
