# Linger 0.4.9: room for a raid, and every emoji

Voice holds a raid now: 60 people in a room, and when a dozen cheer at once
you hear the six loudest instead of a wall of noise. Every room sounds better
too, because a lost packet no longer cuts a voice out for everybody. The emoji
picker has every emoji there is, Discord's `:shortcodes:` work, and a host can
add the server's own. Rooms stop showing as new when you've read them, and a
person who dropped out of voice stops showing up there two or three times.

**Hosts: update the server.** The voice fixes, emoji and read positions all
need it. **This one changes the database**, so going back to 0.4.8 means
restoring the backup `update.sh` makes. **A server with no voice address set
turns voice on** after this update. [For hosts](#for-hosts-updating-the-server) has
the steps.

**Everyone: update the app.** A raid wants everybody in voice on 0.4.9.
Older apps keep sending silence, which costs the server, and when seven or
more people talk at once, an older app's voice is the first left out.

## The highlights

- **A raid fits in one voice room** (#197). Up to 60 people, up from 25. Apps
  send nothing while you're quiet, and the server passes on the six loudest
  voices at once, so twelve people cheering when the boss goes down still
  sounds like a crowd and doesn't swamp the server.
- **A lost packet doesn't stop a voice** (#438). One packet lost on the way to
  the server made that person go silent for about a third of a second, for
  everybody in the room. That has happened in every voice room since 0.4.1.
- **Every emoji, and a server's own** (#359). See [Emoji](#emoji).
- **Rooms you've read stop looking new** (#453, #454), on the phone after
  it's been put away and on your other devices after you send something.
- **You show up in voice once** (#451). A friend showed as "you, you, you"
  in a room's voice, because connections that had dropped kept their seats.

## Voice

- **Sixty people a room** (#197), up from 25. Your app sends nothing while
  you're quiet, muted or not pressing push-to-talk, the way Discord's does. It
  used to send fifty packets of silence a second, and the server copied every
  one to everybody.
- **The six loudest voices** (#197). However many people talk at once, the
  server passes on the six loudest. Somebody already talking keeps their
  place unless a newcomer is clearly louder, so nobody is cut off mid-word.
- **Better sound in rooms of up to twenty** (#431). Everybody sends at
  128 kbit/s, more than twice what voice used before. From twenty-one people
  the room steps down to 96 kbit/s, and back up once it's down to sixteen.
  Nobody sets it, and nobody hears the switch.
- **A lost packet doesn't stop a voice** (#438). See the highlights. In a big
  room it got worse fast: with 60 people and 8 talking, listeners heard 78%
  of what was said. Now it's 99%.
- **A room filling all at once could leave somebody hearing nobody** (#197).
  In about one in ten joins of 60 people at once, one person's app refused
  the server's call setup and heard silence. Fixed.
- **The voice bar in a big room** (#197). With more than six people, it
  keeps six places: you, then whoever just talked, lit while they talk.
  Somebody new who talks takes the place of whoever spoke longest ago, so
  nobody jumps around while talking. Everyone in the room is a small dot in
  their own color underneath, ringed while they talk. Click the dots to see
  everyone by name, type to find somebody, and press **Enter** to set their
  volume. A room of six or fewer looks as it did.
- **You show up in voice once** (#451). See the highlights. Behind the scenes,
  your other connections were sometimes hung up on as well, and reconnected
  so fast nobody noticed.
- **A microphone or speaker that stops working** (#442) waits its half second
  before Linger opens it again. A second report of the same stop could cut
  that wait short, and the microphone could open once more just after you
  left voice.

## The list on a big server

- **People shows the people you talk to** (#197), on a server with more than
  twelve people here or away: anybody you've ever had a DM with. Everyone
  else folds under **Everyone else**, which starts folded. A smaller server's
  list is unchanged.
- **A busy room's row shows up to 16 dots**, up from 5, so a full raid looks
  full.
- **One line per person**, a new switch in Settings → Appearance, off to
  start. Every row in the list is one line, and a status shows when you point
  at somebody. The phone keeps two lines.

## Emoji

- **Every emoji there is** (#359), up from 66. Search by name or by a word
  for it ("hello" finds 👋), jump to a group with the tabs, and the emoji you
  use come back at the top. The hand beside the search sets a skin tone once.
- **Shortcodes.** Type a colon and two letters, `:smi`, and pick from the
  list, the way `@` offers people. A whole name, `:smiley:`, turns into 😃 as
  you type it. The names are Discord's (`:thumbsup:`, `:slight_smile:`), and
  GitHub's and Slack's work too.
- **A message of just a few emoji is drawn big**, as Discord does.
- **A server's own emoji.** The host (or a co-host) adds pictures in
  **Settings → Emoji**, and each becomes an emoji at once, named from its file:
  `party-parrot.gif` is `:party_parrot:`. Animated GIFs stay animated. Up to
  200 a server.
- Emoji are drawn by your computer's own emoji font, so they look a little
  different on Windows and Linux, and an older Windows 10 leaves out the
  newest few dozen.

## Reading

- **Coming back to the phone app no longer lights up nearly every room**
  (#453). The app asked the server how far you'd read once per connection,
  and if that one ask failed, every room looked new until a restart. A phone
  coming back from the background often asked before Android had let its
  network back in. It now asks again, and asks after every fresh connection
  too.
- **Your own message no longer shows as new on your other devices** (#454).
  Sending a message now marks it read on the server, for every device you
  use. A phone put away straight after sending used to lose that.
- **How far you've read only moves forward.** One device can no longer pull
  back what another one read.

## Linux

- **Opening Linger while it's already running no longer turns the GPU path
  off** (#447). Opening it again from the menu left a marker behind that the
  next start took for a crash, so it switched WebKit's faster drawing off.
  Typing then ran a beat behind, and on a computer with two graphics chips the
  window could go grey. **If typing has felt slow since**, delete the
  `gbm-off-…` file in `~/.local/state/linger/` and open Linger again.
- **A computer with two graphics chips shows the app, not a grey window**
  (#433). With WebKit's GPU path off (the AppImage always has it off), the
  page could draw on one chip while the window was on the other. Linger now
  sends the picture through ordinary memory in that case, which either chip
  can show.

## Under the hood

- The release's Android check reads the signer the way the build machine
  prints it, and shows what it read when it can't (T-1604). 0.4.8's check
  failed on that, so its `.apk` was built by hand. This one comes from the
  release run.
- CI's final check fails when GitHub never ran a job, instead of passing
  (#445).
- The README has badges, a link to the website, and the website is step 5 of
  a release.

## Known issues

- **Read positions don't move live between two devices that are both open.**
  Read a room on the phone while the computer is open, and the computer shows
  it as new until it next connects afresh (a restart, say). Your own messages
  are fine either way.
- **The phone app doesn't update itself.** Install each new `.apk` over the
  old one.
- **On Hyprland (Omarchy included), the mouse pointer jumps into a
  conversation that opens in a window of its own.** The
  [user guide](../user-guide.md#worth-knowing) has the two lines that turn it
  off.
- **The AppImage doesn't play AV1 videos** (#358). Windows and the Arch,
  `.deb` and `.rpm` packages do.

## For hosts: updating the server

Server image: `ghcr.io/itsmattguenther/linger:0.4.9` (x86-64 only), also
tagged `latest`.

**What changes for you:**

- **Voice turns on by itself, if it was off.** A server works out where voice
  goes from its name (`LINGER_DOMAIN`) now. If you never set
  `LINGER_VOICE_ADDRESS`, your server had no voice and now has it, as long as
  UDP 3479 is open ([Voice](../host-guide.md#voice) in the host guide). To
  keep voice off, set `LINGER_VOICE_ADDRESS=off` (in `.env`, or in the
  `environment:` part of an older `compose.yaml`) before updating. If you set
  an address already, nothing changes.
- **A database change** (server emoji). `update.sh` saves the database into
  `backups/` before it starts 0.4.9, and that backup is how you'd go back.
- **Server emoji** are yours (and your co-hosts') to add in Settings → Emoji.
  Friends on an older app see them as `:name:` until they update.
- **A raid needs everybody on 0.4.9.** The server now throws away the silence
  older apps send, but they still send it, and that costs your server. An
  older app also can't say how loud it is, so when seven or more people talk
  at once, its voice is the first left out. Upload for fifty people stays near
  35 Mbps however many talk. The [host guide](../host-guide.md#voice) has the
  numbers.
- **The data folder fixes itself.** The image makes `data` writable on its
  own, so no more `chown` after setting up or moving a server.
- **Setting up a new server is one script**, `deploy/setup.sh`, and every
  setting lives in `.env`. An older `compose.yaml` with its settings written
  inside keeps working, and nothing makes you move. The host guide says how,
  if you'd like to ([Moving an older server's settings into
  `.env`](../host-guide.md#updating-the-server)).

Run these **on the server**, one block at a time.

**1. Go to the folder with your `compose.yaml`** (the `linger` folder from
setup):

```bash
cd ~/linger
```

**2. Get this release's `update.sh`.** It now says where voice goes; an
older copy warns that a server with no voice address carries no voice, which
isn't true from 0.4.9.

```bash
curl -fLO https://raw.githubusercontent.com/itsMattGuenther/Linger/main/deploy/update.sh
chmod +x update.sh
```

**3. Update:**

```bash
./update.sh
```

(Use `sudo ./update.sh` if you type `sudo` before `docker` commands.) It
says where voice goes, downloads 0.4.9 while the server keeps running, stops
it for a few seconds to save the database into `backups/`, starts everything
again, and prints the version before and after.

**4. Check it worked:**

```bash
curl -f https://linger.example.com/api/v1/health
```

With your own address in place of `linger.example.com`, it should print
`{"ok":true,"version":"0.4.9"}`. Then `docker compose logs linger | grep -i voice`
says where voice goes, or that it's off.

**If something goes wrong**, `update.sh` prints the commands that put 0.4.8
back **with the backup it just made**. Going back needs that backup: 0.4.8
won't start on a database 0.4.9 has changed. Anything sent after the update
is lost when you go back, so decide soon if you're going to.

## Install or update

Use the in-app update: **Update…** at the foot of the list, or Settings →
Account & App → Updates. Windows users should keep the same installer format
(`.exe` or `.msi`) when upgrading. Linux packages are `.AppImage`, `.deb` and
`.rpm`; on Omarchy or Arch, Linger updates with your system. Windows
installers are updater-signed, not Authenticode-signed; SmartScreen may still
warn about an unfamiliar app.

On Android, download `Linger_0.4.9_android-arm64.apk` from this release on
the phone and install it over the old one. You stay signed in. A first install
is step by step in the [0.4.8 notes](0.4.8.md#the-phone-app).

## Checks on installed clients

Record OS, package type, previous and new version, and headset or microphone
with any failure. Use made-up messages, and keep private conversations out of
screenshots.

1. **Voice across two real networks** (a home connection and a phone hotspot,
   say), with three or more people: everybody hears everybody, and a
   talker's voice doesn't drop out in short gaps (#438, #431).
2. **A big room**, with more than six people in voice: the voice bar keeps six
   places, nobody moves while they talk, the dots light, and the everyone card
   finds a person by name (#197).
3. **Two devices in voice, then cut one's network**: its seat goes within
   about four minutes, the other device stays connected, and the chat's
   voice line shows that person once (#451).
4. **Phone: read everything on the computer**, leave the phone app in the
   background for a few minutes, come back: nothing shows as new. Send from
   the phone and put it away at once: the computer doesn't show that room as
   new (#453, #454).
5. **Emoji:** find one by a word in the picker, type `:thumbsup:` in the box,
   and send three emoji alone. As the host, add a server emoji in
   Settings → Emoji and send it; somebody else sees the picture (#359).
6. **Linux, a package:** open Linger from the menu while it runs, quit, open
   again. `~/.local/state/linger/` holds only `running.lock` (#447).
7. **Linux, two graphics chips, the AppImage:** the window shows the app
   (#433).
8. **As a host with no voice address set:** after `update.sh`, the logs say
   where voice goes, and a call works (#440).
