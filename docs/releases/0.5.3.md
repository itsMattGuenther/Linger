# Linger 0.5.3: screen sharing, fixed on Linux and with sound

Screen sharing came out in 0.5.2, and the first week of real use found what
it got wrong. On Linux, watching a screen failed on computers with NVIDIA's
video drivers, and crashed the app on some others; both are fixed. Sharing
from Linux was hard on the computer, and now uses the graphics card. Share's
two choices are **High quality** and **Low quality**, saying what each sends.
The person sharing hears a soft sound when somebody starts watching. And
from Windows, the computer's own sound can go with the picture: a video, a
game, a clip in a slide, without the call's voices coming back.

**Everyone: update the app.** Every fix is in the app.

**Hosts: update the server** with `./update.sh`, as usual, **for the
computer's sound in a share**. Everything else in this release works with a
0.5.2 server. There's no database change, and nothing in `compose.yaml`
changes. See [For hosts](#for-hosts-updating-the-server).

## The highlights

- **Watching works on Linux** with NVIDIA's drivers (#730), and a computer
  that can't play a screen says so instead of closing (#729).
- **High quality or Low quality** (#732): 1080p at 60 fps, or 720p at 30 fps.
  **Low quality is now 720p**, smaller than 0.5.2's 30 fps choice.
- **Share the computer's sound** from Windows (#736), off until you turn it on.
- **Sharing from Linux is quieter** (#733): the graphics card makes the
  picture, and nothing is made while nobody watches.
- **A sound when somebody starts watching** your share (#735).
- **A clearer note for a black share** (#731).

## Watching on Linux

- **"This device can't play" on Linux, fixed** (#730). Where NVIDIA's video
  drivers, or VA-API's for AMD and Intel, had put hardware video decoders on
  the computer, the Linux app's web engine picked them, and they refused the
  picture Linger sends. Every screen said "This device can't play". The app
  now plays screens with the web engine's own decoder, the one that always
  worked. If you set `GST_PLUGIN_FEATURE_RANK` yourself to get round it,
  you can take it out; if you keep it, yours still wins.
- **No more closing the moment you watch** (#729). On a computer where
  Linger draws without the graphics card, the screen's player closed the
  whole app. That's computers with NVIDIA's older "legacy" driver (a GTX 980
  or 1080, say), and anybody who starts Linger with `GDK_GL=disable`. Playing
  a screen needs the graphics card, so on those computers the screen now
  says "This device can't play Jules's screen". Watch on a phone instead.
  Sharing from them works.

## Share your screen

- **High quality or Low quality** (#732). Share's two choices said "Sharp
  text" and "Smooth motion", which read like they'd change how a whole 4K
  screen looked. They now say what they send:
  - **High quality**, 1080p at 60 fps, first in the menu. Pick it to present:
    slides, documents, code, video.
  - **Low quality**, 720p at 30 fps, lighter on everybody's connection and
    on your computer. **This is smaller than 0.5.2's 30 fps choice**, which
    sent up to 1080p: for small text, pick High quality.
  - A window smaller than that is sent at its own size either way.
- **Share the computer's sound** (#736), from the Windows app on Windows 10
  version 2004 or later. Tick it in the Share menu, under High and Low
  quality. It's off until you turn it on, and your computer remembers it.
  - The people watching hear what your computer plays, and never the call:
    Linger leaves its own sounds out, everybody's voices included, so nobody
    hears themselves come back.
  - A small speaker after "Sharing your screen" says the sound is going. If
    it couldn't be shared, the box says so, and the picture goes on without
    it.
  - The Linux app shares the picture alone for now. It can't yet leave its
    own voice out of what your desktop plays.
  - The tick shows only where the server passes the sound on: a server
    updated to 0.5.3.
- **Hearing a shared screen.** A screen with sound has a speaker beside Full
  screen: press it to turn the sound off, and again to bring it back. On a
  computer, a slider beside it sets how loud, through the speakers you
  picked for voice. Deafen silences it with the voices. With the speaker
  off, the server stops sending it to you. On a phone, the phone's own
  buttons set how loud, and a phone whose web engine can't play the sound
  says so on the speaker.
- **A sound when somebody starts watching** (#735), different from somebody
  joining voice, so you know without looking at the box. Only you hear it,
  at most once a minute for each person, and never when somebody stops
  watching. If they pressed Watch from outside voice, you hear just this
  one, not the join sound too. It's on until you turn it off: **Settings →
  Sound & Voice → Someone starts watching your screen**. Quiet hours leave
  it on, like the other voice sounds.
- **A black share says what to do** (#731). When a share goes black, its box
  now says: "Everyone watching saw a black screen. If you're showing slides,
  stop sharing, then share again and pick Entire screen. PowerPoint plays a
  slide show in a window of its own, so sharing only the PowerPoint window
  comes out black." The desktop notice says the same, shorter.

## Sharing from Linux, lighter

- **The graphics card makes the picture** (#733), where it can: NVIDIA's
  H.264 encoder, or VA-API's on AMD and Intel. 0.5.2 made it on the
  processor, which is what spun the fans up and made the pointer skip. On a
  busy 4K screen at High quality, Linger went from about 2 of the
  processor's cores to about 1, and sent a sharper picture.
  - NVIDIA's cards need nothing more.
  - AMD's and Intel's need GStreamer's VA-API plugin: `gst-plugin-va` on
    Arch and Omarchy, `gstreamer1.0-plugins-bad` on Debian and Ubuntu,
    `gstreamer1-plugins-bad-free` on Fedora. The .deb and .rpm recommend it.
  - Without one, Linger makes the picture on the processor, as before.
- **Nothing is made while nobody watches.** 0.5.2 made the picture the whole
  time you shared, watched or not.

## Under the hood

- **Every change now checks that a screen plays in the packaged Linux app**,
  on Ubuntu's web engine and on Arch's, the one Omarchy runs. 0.5.2's checks
  ran with the web engine's graphics-card drawing switched off, which is
  exactly where watching broke. Another check makes sure a computer that
  draws without the graphics card says "can't play" rather than closing.
- **Each release is now checked on a real graphics card** before it ships.
  GitHub's machines have none, so the release steps run the packaged app on
  a computer with NVIDIA and AMD cards, their hardware decoders installed.
  Run against 0.5.2, these checks fail on both of its Linux bugs.

## Known issues

- **The computer's sound is new.** It was tested on GitHub's Windows
  machines: the app captured sound, sent it, and every app played it. Those
  machines have no speakers, so a real share of a video, with people
  talking in the call, hasn't been heard yet. Tell us if anybody hears
  themselves come back.
- **The sound on an iPhone** hasn't been tried. Its web engine may not be
  able to play it; if not, the speaker says so.
- **Picture and sound can be slightly out of step.** The sound plays about a
  tenth of a second behind on a computer. Each packet carries the time it
  was captured, so a later release can line the two up exactly.
- **The Linux app doesn't share the computer's sound**, the AppImage doesn't
  share at all, and a Mac and the phones only watch.
- **A computer with NVIDIA's legacy driver can't watch a screen** (#729).

## Older apps and servers

Nothing stops working with an older app or server (PROTOCOL §9).

- **A 0.5.2 app on a 0.5.3 server** works as before. It doesn't offer the
  computer's sound, and hears none.
- **A 0.5.3 app on a 0.5.2 server** shares and watches as before, with every
  fix in this release. It doesn't offer **Share the computer's sound**
  there: the server doesn't pass sound on.
- **A 0.5.2 app watching a 0.5.3 share with sound** sees the picture and
  hears nothing: the server sends sound only to an app that asks for it.
- **To hear a shared screen's sound**, the person sharing, the person
  watching and the server all need 0.5.3.

## For hosts: updating the server

Server image: `ghcr.io/itsmattguenther/linger:0.5.3` (x86-64 only), also
tagged `0.5` and `latest`.

### What changes for you

- **The server passes a shared screen's sound on**, to the people watching
  who have their speaker on. It's about 128 kbps more for each of them,
  next to the picture's 2.5 to 6 Mbps, and it counts toward the 10 Mbps
  your server holds every share to. An hour of a game shared with sound to
  eight people is about half a gigabyte more than without.
- **No new setting.** `LINGER_SCREENS=off` turns the sound off with the
  screens.
- **No database change**, and nothing in `compose.yaml` changes.

### The steps

On the server, in your `linger` folder:

```bash
cd ~/linger
./update.sh
```

(Use `sudo ./update.sh` if you type `sudo` before `docker` commands.) It
downloads 0.5.3 while the server keeps running, stops it for a few seconds to
save the database into `backups/`, then starts everything again and prints the
version before and after. If 0.5.3 doesn't start, it prints the commands that
put 0.5.2 back.

Check it worked, with your own address in place of `linger.example.com`:

```bash
curl -f https://linger.example.com/api/v1/health
```

It prints `{"ok":true,"version":"0.5.3"}`.

**Going back to 0.5.2** is safe: the database is the same, so the commands
`update.sh` prints are all it takes.

## Install or update

Use the in-app update: **Update…** at the foot of the list, or Settings →
Account & App → Updates. Windows users should keep the same installer format
(`.exe` or `.msi`) when upgrading. Linux packages are `.AppImage`, `.deb` and
`.rpm`; on Omarchy or Arch, Linger updates with your system (Update in the
Omarchy menu, or `sudo pacman -Syu`). Windows installers are updater-signed,
not Authenticode-signed; SmartScreen may still warn about an unfamiliar app.

On Android, download `Linger_0.5.3_android-arm64.apk` from this release on
the phone and install it over the old one. You stay signed in. A first install
is step by step in the [0.4.8 notes](0.4.8.md#the-phone-app). On an iPhone,
TestFlight offers 0.5.3 once it's through Apple's processing.

## Checks on installed clients

Record OS, package type, previous and new version, and what was shared, with
any failure. Use made-up slides and messages, and keep private windows out of
what you share.

1. **Watch on Linux with NVIDIA's drivers**, with no `GST_PLUGIN_FEATURE_RANK`
   set: a share from Windows and one from Linux both play (#730).
2. **Watch on a computer with NVIDIA's legacy driver**: the screen says it
   can't play, and Linger stays open (#729).
3. **Share from Linux at High quality**, a whole screen, while somebody
   watches: the fans stay quiet and the pointer doesn't skip (#733).
4. **High and Low quality** from Windows and from Linux: High is sharp at
   1080p, Low is 720p (#732).
5. **The computer's sound from Windows**: tick it, share a video, and talk
   in the call while it plays. Watched on Windows, on Linux and on an
   Android phone, everybody hears the video and nobody hears themselves
   back. The speaker button and the slider work, and Deafen silences it
   (#736).
6. **The sound tick off**: a Windows share is as it was in 0.5.2 (#736).
7. **Somebody starts watching**: the person sharing hears the new sound,
   once, and not again when that person watches again within a minute
   (#735).
8. **A black share**: share only the PowerPoint window and start the slide
   show; the box says what to do (#731).
9. **iPhone, through TestFlight**: watch a share with sound; it plays, or
   the speaker says it can't.
